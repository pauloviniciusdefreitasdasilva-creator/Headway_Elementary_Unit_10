🔥 Warm-Up — What are you up to?

Start with spontaneous answers. Ask one or two follow-ups for each prompt; note errors with am/is/are and verb-ing, then give feedback after the learner finishes.

1. What are you wearing today?

Describe at least three items or colors.

What color is each item? Why are you wearing those clothes? What do you usually wear to school that is different from today?

2. What do you usually do after school?

Use Present Simple to describe your routine.

What time do you get home? Do you usually do homework first? What are you doing after school today?

3. What are your friends or family doing right now?

Use complete sentences with am/is/are + verb-ing.

Who is doing something different today? Is anyone working, studying, cooking, or relaxing? How do you know?

4. What do you usually do at the weekend?

Contrast a usual activity with a temporary plan.

Where do you usually go? Who do you spend time with? Are you doing anything special this weekend?

5. Imagine you are at a busy shopping mall. What can you see?

Describe at least three people and what they are doing.

What are they wearing? Who is carrying a bag? Is anyone talking on the phone, buying something, or looking for a friend?

6. What are you doing differently this week?

Use the Present Continuous for a temporary change or activity around now.

Are you studying something new? Are you preparing for a test? How is this week different from a typical week?

Quick teacher check

Listen for: subject + am/is/are + verb-ing for actions in progress, and Present Simple for habits. Encourage an extra detail with because, but, or so.

📘 Grammar Master — Present Continuous vs Present Simple

Unit 10 focus: actions happening now, temporary activity, questions about what people are doing, and the contrast with routines and facts.

1. Present Continuous — now / around now

Form: subject + am/is/are + verb-ing

I’m studying now.
She’s talking on the phone.
They’re having a barbecue.

now · right now · at the moment

2. Present Simple — habits / facts

Form: subject + base verb (add -s/-es for he/she/it)

I usually study after school.
He works in an office.
They live in London.

usually · often · every day

3. Negative forms

Present Continuous: I’m not working. / She isn’t sleeping. / They aren’t waiting.

Present Simple: I don’t work on Sundays. / He doesn’t wear a uniform.

After doesn’t, use the base verb: doesn’t wear (not doesn’t wears).

4. Questions

Present Continuous: Are you listening? What is she doing?

Present Simple: Do you work here? Where does he live?

Continuous: am/is/are before the subject. Simple: do/does before the subject.

5. Spelling the -ing form

work → working · make → making · run → running · lie → lying

Usually add -ing; drop silent -e; double a final consonant in many short vowel + consonant verbs; change -ie to -y.

6. Verbs that usually describe states

I know the answer. · She likes art.

At this level, verbs such as know and like are normally used in the Present Simple when describing a state.

Correction priorities

Form

Check the correct form of am/is/are, spelling of -ing, negatives, and subject–auxiliary order in questions.

Meaning

Ask “Is this a routine/fact or something happening now/temporarily?” before deciding which tense fits.

Possession

Contrast a noun phrase with a possessive pronoun: “Anton’s hat” → “his”; “Charlotte’s glasses” → “hers”.

Social language

Check whether the response fits the situation: sympathy, reassurance, congratulations, invitations, polite refusal, or saying goodbye.
